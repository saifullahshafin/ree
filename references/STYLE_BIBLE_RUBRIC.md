# Style bible rubric

A style bible answers every section below from the pack's data. Tag each claim `[measured n/N, v/V videos]`
or `[interpreted: <evidence>]`. If the data cannot answer a section, say so; do not fill it from general knowledge
about the creator.

## 1. Identity in one paragraph
What a viewer would recognise with the sound off. Then the 5 signature moves, ranked by frequency.

## 2. Pacing and structure
- Change points per minute, edits per minute, shot length median and p25 to p75, longest stretch with no edit.
- Hook (first 60 s) density vs body. How the hook is built (montage? credentials? promise card?).
- Pace curve across the video: where it speeds up and slows down, and what the speech is doing there.
- Chapter or section markers and how sections are separated.

## 3. Layout grammar (screen states)
- Share of time in each layout (talking head wide/medium/close, punch-in, PIP, split, full-screen graphic, B-roll).
- **When and why each layout switches**, as trigger rules: which talk beats lead to PIP, which to full-screen
  graphics, which back to the face. Typical hold time per layout.
- PIP geometry: side, size, corner radius, border, background behind it.
- Zoom and punch-in use: how often, by what amount, on which words or energy.

## 4. Graphics vocabulary
For each recurring graphic kind (text card, chart, icon pop, screenshot, chat bubble, diagram, kinetic text...):
frequency, the talk beat and spoken pattern that triggers it, lag from trigger word to appearance, hold time,
entrance and exit motion, sound, and the spec file that rebuilds it.
- **2D vs 2.5D vs 3D:** counts, and exactly where depth is used (what kind of moment earns it).
- **Data graphics:** chart types, how numbers are animated (counters, bars growing), lag after the spoken number.

## 5. Emotion and energy mapping
- Cross-table speaker energy x edit type. What the edit does when he is calm, emphatic, playful, vulnerable.
- Story and personal moments: does the edit get quieter (fewer graphics, closer framing) or louder?
- Jokes and punchlines: meme clips, sound effects, freeze frames, zooms?

## 6. Typography, colour and brand
- Typefaces by role (title cards, captions, labels, numbers): serif/sans, weight, case, size as % of frame.
- Measured palette from specs (hex with share), backgrounds, accent colours, highlight colours.
- Shapes: card radius, pills, bubbles, shadows, borders. Logo or brand marks. Recurring backgrounds/textures.

## 7. Motion language
Entrance types and their measured durations and easing, stagger patterns, exits, transitions between scenes.
What never happens (e.g. no 3D flips, no glitch) is as useful as what does.

## 8. Sound design
Music presence and character by section, ducking under speech, sound effects per motion type and how tightly
they sync, silence used as a beat.

## 9. B-roll and evidence
Self-shot vs stock vs screenshots vs screen recordings; what claims get visual proof (testimonials, websites,
books, studies) and how proof is framed (highlighting, zoom on text, blur on the rest).

## 10. Replication checklist
A numbered list an editor follows on a fresh talking-head recording to make it feel like this creator,
ordered from biggest impact to smallest, each item pointing to its recipe and rule.

## 11. Confidence and gaps
Verification sample results, known label error types, and what needs a human look.
